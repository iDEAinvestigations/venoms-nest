GPS Trackers & Skimmers Detection

This guide covers detecting hidden GPS trackers, card skimmers, Bluetooth devices, and wireless surveillance equipment.

Overview​

The GPS Trackers & Skimmers section helps detect suspicious wireless devices that may be:

  • GPS Trackers - Hidden devices tracking your location
  • Card Skimmers - Devices stealing credit card information
  • Bluetooth Trackers - AirTags, Tile trackers on your devices
  • Hidden Devices - Bluetooth/BLE devices with suspicious signals
  • Rogue Access Points - Fake Wi-Fi networks for interception

Quick Start​

1. Open GPS Trackers & Skimmers Tab​

From main menu, tap GPS Trackers & Skimmers tab

2. Run a Comprehensive Scan​

Tap "SCAN ALL NATIVE" button to:

  • Scan Wi-Fi networks
  • Scan Bluetooth devices
  • Scan Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) devices
  • Identify suspicious devices automatically

Scan typically takes 30-60 seconds

3. Review Results​

Results display in two sections:

  • Suspicious Networks - Rogue Wi-Fi access points
  • Suspicious Devices - Tracking devices or hidden hardware

Hardware Status Panel​

At the top of the screen, RF Scorpion shows available hardware:

HardwarePurposeStatus

BluetoothDetect classic Bluetooth✅ Available / ❌ Not Available
BLEDetect Bluetooth Low Energy✅ Available / ❌ Not Available
NFCDetect NFC readers✅ Available / ❌ Not Available

info

All modern Android phones have Wi-Fi and Bluetooth. NFC is on newer flagship phones only.

Wi-Fi Network Scanning​

What It Detects​

The app identifies suspicious networks designed to intercept data:

High-Risk Networks:

  • Networks named "FREE", "PUBLIC", "OPEN"
  • Networks named "ATM", "POS", "SQUARE", "CREDIT"
  • Hidden networks with very strong signals
  • Networks mimicking legitimate business names

How to Use:

  1. Tap "SCAN WI-FI" button
  2. Wait for scan to complete
  3. Review list of networks found
  4. Red flags indicate suspicious networks

Interpreting Results​

FlagMeaningRisk Level
🔴 Suspicious NameNetwork designed to look legitimateHIGH
🔴 Very Strong SignalUnusually powerful hidden networkHIGH
🟡 Open NetworkNo password encryptionMEDIUM
🟢 Known NetworkRecognized business/carrierLOW

What to Do if Suspicious Network Found​

  • ⚠️ Do NOT connect to suspicious networks
  • 📸 Take screenshot for documentation
  • 🗺️ Note location using maps
  • 📞 Report to authorities if in public space
  • 🔒 Use VPN on public Wi-Fi if necessary

Bluetooth Device Scanning​

What It Detects​

Tracking Devices:

  • Apple AirTags
  • Tile trackers
  • TrackR devices
  • Samsung SmartTags

Suspicious Devices:

  • Devices with "GPS", "Tracker", "TAG" in name
  • Devices with unusually strong signals
  • Hidden devices connecting without user permission
  • Devices with unknown manufacturers

How to Use​

Scan All Bluetooth:

  1. Tap "SCAN BLUETOOTH" button
  2. Wait for scan (30-45 seconds)
  3. Review discovered devices

Individual Device Check:

  1. Tap "SCAN BLUETOOTH"
  2. Look through results
  3. Tap any device for more info:
    • Device name
    • MAC address
    • Signal strength (RSSI)
    • Manufacturer
    • Device type (if known)

Identifying Your Own Devices​

Your Known Devices Will Show:

  • ✅ Device names you recognize
  • ✅ Expected signal strengths
  • ✅ Known manufacturers (Apple, Samsung, etc.)
  • ✅ Devices synced to your phone

Unknown Devices May Indicate:

  • ❌ Hidden tracking devices
  • ❌ Neighboring devices
  • ❌ Malicious equipment

Signal Strength Reference​

RSSIApproximate DistanceMeaning
-30 to -50 dBm1-3 feetVery close, strong tracker
-50 to -70 dBm3-30 feetNearby device
-70 to -90 dBm30-100 feetDistant device
Below -90 dBm100+ feetFar away or low power

Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) Scanning​

What It Detects​

BLE is used by modern tracking devices:

Common BLE Trackers:

  • Apple AirTags
  • Tile Slim/Pro
  • Chipolo
  • TrackR Bravo
  • Samsung SmartTag

How to Use​

  1. Tap "SCAN BLE" button
  2. Wait for scan to complete
  3. Review device list
  4. Check for unknown trackers

Findings Explained​

Each BLE device shows:

  • Device Name - Name of tracker
  • MAC Address - Unique identifier
  • RSSI - Signal strength (distance indicator)
  • Manufacturer - Company that made it
  • TX Power - Transmission strength
warning

If you find unknown AirTags or Tile devices nearby, and multiple scan results show the same device, it may be tracking you. Note location and consider changing route.

Advanced Features​

Engineering Menu​

Tap "ENGINEERING MENU" to open your phone's diagnostic interface:

  • Shows cellular information
  • Displays network stats
  • Access to advanced diagnostics
  • Phone manufacturer dependent

SDR Integration​

For advanced tracking detection beyond phone capabilities:

What is SDR?

  • Software Defined Radio
  • External hardware connected via USB
  • Can detect GPS trackers on 1.2-1.6 GHz
  • Can detect ISM band devices (315/433/868/915 MHz)

Supported Hardware:

  • RTL-SDR ($25-30)
  • HackRF One ($299)
  • USRP devices (higher cost)

To Connect SDR:

  1. Purchase SDR hardware
  2. Get USB OTG adapter for your phone
  3. Connect SDR via USB to phone
  4. Tap "SDR INFO" for instructions
  5. Follow on-screen guidance

Best Practices​

Regular Scanning​

  • At Home: Weekly baseline check
  • Before Traveling: Scan before leaving
  • In Hotels: Scan room upon arrival
  • At Workplaces: Monthly security checks
  • In Vehicles: Check for hidden trackers

What to Document​

When you find suspicious devices:

  1. Take screenshots
  2. Note exact location
  3. Record time and date
  4. Document device name and signals
  5. Save for security team review

Physical Inspection​

After finding suspicious signals:

  • Check vehicle for hidden devices (wheel wells, OBD port)
  • Inspect home for unusual electronics
  • Look under furniture and fixtures
  • Check electrical outlets
  • Examine high-value items (luggage, bags)

Limitations & Important Notes​

⚠️ Limited Range

  • Phone Bluetooth range: typically 30-100 feet
  • Cannot detect all remote trackers
  • Long-range trackers may not show signals

⚠️ Frequency Limitations

  • Phone Wi-Fi: 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz only
  • Phone Bluetooth: 2.4 GHz band only
  • GPS trackers often use other frequencies (1.2-1.6 GHz)
  • ISM band devices (315/433/868/915 MHz) not detectable without SDR

⚠️ False Positives

  • Neighboring devices may show up
  • Legitimate devices in range will appear
  • Use context to identify real threats

Troubleshooting​

Scan finds no devices:

  • Ensure Bluetooth/Wi-Fi enabled
  • Check Android permissions granted
  • Try moving to different location
  • Restart RF Scorpion

Known device not showing:

  • Device may be turned off
  • Device may be out of range
  • Bluetooth may need to be re-enabled
  • Try scanning again
