Tower Survey - Cellular Signal Detection & Analysis

The Tower Survey is RF Scorpion's primary feature for detecting and analyzing cellular tower signals, including identification of potential fake cell towers (Stingrays/IMSI Catchers).

How Tower Survey Works​

Your Android device constantly receives signals from nearby cellular towers on downlink frequencies (what towers send to phones). The Tower Survey analyzes these signals to detect anomalies that may indicate surveillance equipment.

What You Can Detect​

✅ Normal Cellular Towers - Legitimate towers from AT&T, Verizon, T-Mobile, and US Cellular ✅ Fake Cell Towers (Stingrays) - Cell site simulators that mimic legitimate towers ✅ IMSI Catchers - Devices that capture phone identifiers ✅ Tower Spoofing - Suspicious tower behavior or fake signals ✅ Signal Anomalies - Unusual signal patterns indicating surveillance

Quick Start - Basic Scanning​

1. Open Tower Survey​

From the main menu, tap "Tower Survey" tab

2. Run a Quick Scan​

Three quick scan buttons available:

  • SCAN LTE - Scans all 4G LTE towers in area
  • SCAN 5G - Scans modern 5G NR (New Radio) towers
  • SCAN GSM - Scans legacy 2G networks

To run a scan:

  1. Tap your desired scan button
  2. Wait for the scan to complete (usually 10-30 seconds)
  3. Results will display on screen

3. Review Results​

Results show:

  • Tower Count - Number of towers detected
  • Signal Strength - Color-coded status (Green/Yellow/Orange)
  • Average RSSI - Signal power levels
  • Coverage Area - Geographic distribution

2. Enter Frequency​

Option A - Use Preset:

  • Select band from dropdown
  • Tap "SCAN EARFCN"

Option B - Manual Entry:

  • Enter EARFCN number (E-UTRA Absolute Radio Frequency Channel Number)
  • Tap "SCAN EARFCN"

3. Review Detailed Results​

Results display:

  • RSSI (Received Signal Strength Indicator) - Overall power level (-140 to -30 dBm)
  • RSRP (Reference Signal Received Power) - LTE signal quality
  • RSRQ (Reference Signal Received Quality) - Signal-to-noise ratio
  • EARFCN - Channel number of tower
  • Band Info - Which frequency band detected

Signal Strength Indicators​

The app uses color coding to show signal quality:

Warning Signs of Fake Towers​

⚠️ Unusually Strong Signals (-40 dBm or stronger)

  • Normal towers rarely produce such strong signals unless you're directly next to them
  • If you see strong signals in open areas, may indicate a Stingray nearby

⚠️ Signal Spikes

  • Sudden signal strength increases without moving
  • Can indicate active surveillance device

⚠️ Multiple Towers with Identical Signals

  • Legitimate towers have varying signal strengths
  • Multiple towers with identical RSSI values is suspicious

⚠️ Persistent Connection to Weak Tower

  • Your phone prefers stronger signals
  • Forced connection to weak tower may indicate IMSI catcher

Signal Metrics Explained​

RSSI (Received Signal Strength Indicator)​

  • Measurement: Total received power (-140 to -30 dBm)
  • What it means: How strong the signal is
  • Your phone's perspective: Overall power it receives from tower
  • Why it matters: Tells you approximate distance and signal quality

RSRP (Reference Signal Received Power)​

  • LTE-specific metric: Measures power of reference signals only
  • More accurate than RSSI: Excludes noise from other sources
  • Typical values: -140 to -30 dBm
  • Use: Better indicator of LTE signal quality

RSRQ (Reference Signal Received Quality)​

  • Measurement: Signal-to-noise ratio
  • Range: -20 to 0 dB
  • What it means: Quality of signal relative to interference
  • Better signals: Closer to 0 dB
  • Worse signals: More negative (like -20 dB)

EARFCN (E-UTRA Absolute Radio Frequency Channel Number)​

  • Purpose: Identifies exact channel/frequency
  • Used by: LTE and 5G networks
  • Uniqueness: Each EARFCN corresponds to one specific frequency
  • Why it matters: Can identify which tower/band is connecting you

Best Practices​

When to Scan​

  • Regular Security: Scan weekly in same location for baseline
  • When Traveling: Check hotel rooms and workspaces
  • After Suspicious Events: Scan if you notice tracking activity
  • Before Important Meetings: Verify area is secure

Where to Scan​

  • Home: Compare results over time for consistency
  • Workplace: Identify towers around office
  • Travel: Scan hotel rooms before entering
  • Outdoor Spaces: Identify towers in public areas

Tips for Better Results​

  1. Move to Different Locations: Towers will show different signal strengths
  2. Check Multiple Times: Results can vary by time of day
  3. Note Baseline Signals: Remember normal signal patterns
  4. Document Anomalies: Save screenshots of suspicious results
  5. Use GPS Data: Cross-reference with actual tower locations

Technical Limitations​

⚠️ Downlink Only

  • RF Scorpion scans downlink frequencies (tower to phone)
  • Cannot scan uplink frequencies (phone to tower)
  • This is a hardware limitation of Android devices

⚠️ Carrier Dependent

  • Can only detect frequencies your phone supports
  • Different phones support different bands
  • Older phones may miss newer 5G bands

⚠️ Signal Variability

  • Signal strength changes with movement, weather, building materials
  • Same tower may show different strength at different times
  • Multiple scans recommended for accurate assessment
